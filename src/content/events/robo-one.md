Twice a year, a hall in Yokohama fills with human-sized bipedal robots that walk into a ring and try to knock each other down — the longest-running bipedal robot combat tournament in the world, going since 2002.

## Why It's Interesting

ROBO-ONE is a robot competition built entirely around one hard constraint: every fighter has to be a **two-legged, human-scale humanoid robot**, not a wheeled or treaded machine. Before any robot is allowed to fight, it has to pass a pre-screening test — ten steps in ten seconds, and getting up unassisted from lying face-down and face-up — followed by a two-minute autonomous demonstration judged by a panel. Only then do the actual bouts happen, on day two, fought roughly like K-1 kickboxing: robots trade punches, kicks, and throws, and a match ends when one robot is knocked down, knocked out of the ring, or simply can't get back up. It's a genuinely odd spectacle — part sports event, part engineering showcase, part slapstick, since a fair number of matches end with a very expensive robot faceplanting to loud applause.

The 45th ROBO-ONE, presented by Plant Life Systems, runs September 26-27, 2026 at the Kanagawa Prefectural Youth Center in Yokohama. Saturday is preliminary battles; Sunday is the final tournament plus the 7th ROBO-ONE Beginners, a friendlier sub-event for newer builders competing with more accessible robot kits. The series alternates between Tokyo-area and more remote venues to spread interest around the country, and admission is free — no ticket, no pre-registration, spectators just walk in.

## Best Time to Visit

The 45th tournament is Saturday, September 26 (preliminaries) and Sunday, September 27, 2026 (finals + Beginners). ROBO-ONE runs on this kind of biannual, venue-rotating schedule rather than a fixed annual date, so check the official site for the next event if you're planning further ahead.

## Getting There

The venue is the Kanagawa Prefectural Youth Center in Momijigaoka, Nishi-ku, Yokohama — about an 8-10 minute walk uphill from JR/subway Sakuragicho Station, or roughly 13 minutes from Keikyu Hinodecho Station.
