Every hanabi festival in Japan is a one-night, plan-your-year-around-it event — except this one. Toyako Onsen fires off a full 20-minute show every single night for six months.

## Why It's Interesting

Now in its 45th year, the Long-Run Fireworks are exactly what they sound like: from April 28 through October 31, at 20:45 sharp, a launch boat sets out across Lake Toya and works its way about two kilometers along the onsen town's shoreline, firing roughly 450 shells whose reflections double on the caldera lake's still surface. Because the platform moves, the show literally comes to you — whichever stretch of promenade, footbath, or hotel balcony you're on gets its moment directly offshore. No crowds, no seat reservations, no weather anxiety (it runs rain or shine): it's the lowest-stakes great fireworks experience in the country, and for an August Hokkaido trip it means fireworks are simply guaranteed, any night you're there.

## Best Time to Visit

Any evening from late April through October — in August, sunset around 18:45 leaves the sky fully dark by showtime at 20:45.

## Getting There

JR to Toya Station (about 2 hours from Sapporo) then a 20-minute bus to Toyako Onsen; by car it's under two hours from Sapporo and pairs naturally with Showa Shinzan next door.
