Every year on the last weekend of August, the quiet residential streets around Koenji Station give way to Tokyo's biggest folk-dance festival — a direct transplant of Tokushima's centuries-old Awa Odori tradition.

## Why It's Interesting

Awa Odori is a folk dance with more than 400 years of history, traditionally associated with the Obon season in Tokushima, Shikoku. Since 1957, Koenji has run its own version, and it has grown enormously: the 2026 edition is the 67th, with around 10,000 dancers organized into competing troupes (*ren*) parading a figure-eight route through eight separate dance zones around JR Koenji and Shin-Koenji stations, while an estimated one million spectators pack the sidewalks over the two nights. The dancing itself — a loose, bent-kneed shuffle set to shamisen, taiko drums, and flute, performed by dancers in bright happi coats and straw hats — is infectious enough that several zones actively invite spectators to join in for a few songs (look for the "let's dance" areas).

## Best Time to Visit

The 2026 dates are Saturday, August 29 and Sunday, August 30, with dancing from 5:00 to 8:00pm both evenings.

## Getting There

Directly around JR Koenji Station and Shin-Koenji Station (Tokyo Metro Marunouchi Line); expect the surrounding streets to be closed to traffic and packed for the full three hours.
