A small, dense museum tucked inside Tokyo Dome, a five-minute walk from three different train stations, holding the entire recorded history of Japanese professional baseball.

## Why It's Interesting

Opened in 1959 next to the old Korakuen Stadium — Japan's professional-baseball mecca before Tokyo Dome replaced it in 1988 — the museum moved into the Dome itself the same year and has stayed there since. Inside: a hall of plaques honoring every player inducted into the Japanese Baseball Hall of Fame, uniforms and equipment representing all twelve NPB (Nippon Professional Baseball) teams, a research library, and a hands-on corner where kids can take a swing at a projected pitch from a famous Japanese player. It's a small footprint packed with a century of the sport's history, and it's one of the few Tokyo museums built to share a building with the thing it's about.

## Best Time to Visit

Open Tuesday through Sunday, 10:00–17:00, extending to 18:00 on days Tokyo Dome hosts a professional game — timing a visit around a game day means catching both the museum and the crowd energy building upstairs. Closed December 29–January 1; also closed Mondays, except during spring/summer school breaks and on national holidays that fall on a Monday.

## Getting There

About 5 minutes on foot from Suidobashi Station (JR Chuo-Sobu Line or Toei Mita Line), or a similarly short walk from Korakuen or Kasuga stations. Enter through Tokyo Dome's Gate 21.
