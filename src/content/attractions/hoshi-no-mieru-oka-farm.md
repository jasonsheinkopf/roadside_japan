South of Nasu proper, in the quiet farm town of Nakagawa, **Hoshi no Mieru Oka Nouen** ("Star-Viewing Hill Farm") is a small family vineyard where the business model is refreshingly simple: pick grapes off the vine, pay for what you carry to the scale.

## Why It's Interesting

Across roughly 4,000 square meters the farm grows **14 grape varieties on about 100 vines** — Shine Muscat, Pione, Fujiminori and others — using a natural, chemical-light cultivation method built around cyanobacteria ("pyrrole farming"). Unlike the flat-fee, all-you-can-eat orchards common elsewhere in Japan, picking here is **priced by weight** (roughly ¥1,500–2,000/kg depending on variety), so a family that wants to taste a little of everything pays for exactly that. The name is literal, too: on clear nights the farm doubles as a stargazing spot, grapes by day and constellations after dark.

## Best Time to Visit

**Mid-August through mid-September only** — this is a short, harvest-dependent window, so **call ahead** (0287-96-3076) to confirm the farm is open before making the drive.

## Getting There

There's no train station nearby; **driving** is the realistic option, roughly a 30–40 minute drive from central Nasu or Nasushiobara, with free on-site parking. A taxi from Nasushiobara Shinkansen station is possible but not cheap for a farm visit.
