Running about a kilometer straight out from **JR Asahikawa Station**, **Heiwa-dōri Kaimono Kōen** was built in 1972 as Japan's first permanent, car-free pedestrian shopping street — and over the decades since, the city has turned it into an open-air sculpture gallery.

## Why It's Interesting

Asahikawa is known as a "city of sculpture," and this street is where that reputation is most visible: roughly **100 outdoor sculptures** line the pedestrian mall, from small bronze figures to genuinely monumental pieces. The two hardest to miss are **Zahyō** ("Coordinates"), a roughly 28-meter stainless-steel sculpture shaped like a giant question mark, and **Kaitaku no Imēji** ("Image of Pioneering"), an iron piece about 21 meters tall near the street's shopping-district intersection. Works by noted sculptors including Chūryō Satō are scattered throughout — a genuinely unusual amount of large-scale public art for a street built primarily as a shopping district.

## Best Time to Visit

Open year-round, at any hour — it's a public street, not a ticketed site, and pleasant to walk in any season the weather allows.

## Getting There

Starts immediately outside JR Asahikawa Station and runs to 9-jō-dōri; the whole street is walkable in under 20 minutes, with public parking available nearby for drivers.
