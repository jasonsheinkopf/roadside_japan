The railway came first; the market grew over it anyway. A century later, neither side has budged — so eight times a day, a full-size train threads a wet market at walking pace.

## Why It's Interesting

The stalls of Maeklong market physically occupy the tracks: fish trays, chili sacks, and vegetable baskets sit between the rails, under awnings that overhang the line. When the approach bell sounds, vendors execute a movement they've done thousands of times — awnings fold, trays slide back on rollers, and a corridor exactly one train wide opens through the market. The train grinds through with centimeters of clearance, and before the last carriage clears, the awnings are already swinging back into place. Locals call it *Talat Rom Hup*, the umbrella pull-down market, and the least touristy way to see it is also the best: ride the train in and watch the market swallow you.

## Best Time to Visit

Any day, but check the current train timetable (roughly eight passes daily) and build your visit around one or two — the market between trains is a normal, excellent wet market.

## Getting There

About 70km southwest of Bangkok: direct minivans run from the city, or take the wonderfully slow two-train route via Mahachai with a river ferry crossing in the middle — the final leg arrives through the market itself.
