Where the **Ishikari River** breaks out of the Kamikawa Basin into the Ishikari Plain, it cuts through a gorge of dark green schist that has been carved for over **100 million years**. The Ainu name — **Kamuy Kotan**, "the dwelling place of gods" — comes from a legend that still marks the rock itself.

## Why It's Interesting

The gorge's most striking features are the **"potholes of Kamuy Kotan"** — hollows the current has drilled into the riverbed rock, some up to 5 meters across, designated a natural monument of Asahikawa City. Ainu legend explains them as the footprints of the demon **Nitnekamuy**, chased down through the pass by the god **Samaykur**; sword-cuts from the fight are said to still show in the rock upstream, and the demon's body became the stone across the river. Beyond the legend and geology, Kamuikotan carries real transport history: it was once a treacherous passage for travelers along the river, and later a stop on the Hakodate Main Line — the old station building and a preserved steam locomotive still stand at the site after the line through the gorge was decommissioned.

## Best Time to Visit

Autumn brings out the best foliage against the gorge walls; the site is scenic in the green months too, and open year-round.

## Getting There

About 20–30 minutes by car from JR Asahikawa Station via Route 12/deviation roads, with two free parking lots on site; by bus, take the Fukagawa-bound route from the station to the Kamuikotan stop (roughly 25 minutes).
