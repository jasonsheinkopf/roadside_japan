On a quiet cape north of Pattaya's beach chaos stands a building that has been actively under construction for more than forty years — on purpose, forever.

## Why It's Interesting

The Sanctuary of Truth is over 100 meters tall and made entirely of carved wood, assembled with traditional Thai joinery instead of nails, every square meter of it sculpture: deities, elephants, celestial dancers, and philosophical allegories from Buddhist, Hindu, and Khmer traditions. Its creator, the tycoon Lek Viriyaphant, conceived it as a statement that human truth-seeking never completes — so the building is designed never to be finished. Tropical weather eats the carvings almost as fast as the on-site workshop produces them, and visitors walk through wearing hard hats while carvers work overhead. It's part temple, part folly, part permanent performance.

## Best Time to Visit

Year-round; late afternoon gives golden light off the sea behind the monument. November-February is the coolest, driest window on the eastern seaboard.

## Getting There

In Naklua, 10-15 minutes north of central Pattaya by taxi or songthaew; Pattaya itself is about two hours from Bangkok by bus or car.
