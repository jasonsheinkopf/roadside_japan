Biei's rolling farmland is famous for looking like a patchwork quilt; Shikisai-no-Oka is where somebody deliberately turned fifteen hectares of it into rainbow stripes.

## Why It's Interesting

The name means "hill of four-season colors," and the planting is engineered so ribbon-bands of different flowers — sunflowers, salvia, dahlias, marigolds, lavender, dozens more across the year — run in parallel curves over the hill crests, with the Tokachi mountain range as backdrop. It's unashamedly a spectacle farm, and the infrastructure leans in: a tractor-pulled "Norokko" wagon trundles visitors through the fields, rentable buggies cover the far corners, and a resident alpaca ranch sits at the top. Crucially for a late-summer trip, its peak runs later than Furano's famous lavender — late July through late September the stripes are at their fullest, making it the anchor flower stop for an August visit to the Biei-Furano corridor (and an easy pairing with the nearby Blue Pond).

## Best Time to Visit

Late July through September for the fullest striped displays; August mid-mornings get tour buses, so arrive at opening or late afternoon.

## Getting There

On Biei's "Patchwork Road" driving circuit, about 40 minutes from Furano; by rail, Bibaushi Station on the JR Furano Line is a 25-minute walk or short taxi away.
