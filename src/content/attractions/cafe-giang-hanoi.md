## The accident that became a national drink

In 1946, fresh milk was scarce in Hanoi. Nguyễn Văn Giảng, then a bartender at the five-star Metropole hotel, improvised: he whisked egg yolk with sugar into a dense, silky foam and floated it over strong Vietnamese coffee. The result — cà phê trứng, egg coffee — tasted less like a compromise and more like liquid tiramisu, and it turned into one of Vietnam's signature drinks. Giảng opened his own café the same year.

## Why It's Interesting

This isn't a shop that serves egg coffee; it's *the* shop. Mr. Giảng passed away in 1987 and his children carry the recipe on — the main café at 39 Nguyễn Hữu Huân is run by his son, and the recipe has barely changed: egg yolk, Vietnamese ground coffee, sugar, and a few ingredients the family keeps to itself. The café itself is a classic Old Quarter experience — a doorway you'd walk straight past, a corridor, a staircase, and then a warren of low tables and lower stools full of people having a quiet, custard-topped epiphany.

## Getting There

It's in Hanoi's Old Quarter at 39 Nguyễn Hữu Huân, a short walk northeast of Hoàn Kiếm Lake. Look for the small sign and go down the alley — the café is up the stairs.
