Thailand has hundreds of waterfalls; Erawan is the one whose photos get accused of being photoshopped.

## Why It's Interesting

The falls step down through seven distinct tiers over about a kilometer and a half of jungle trail, and every tier pools into water the color of a swimming-pool advertisement — a genuine mineral effect from the limestone the river dissolves, which also builds the smooth travertine shelves and natural water-slides between pools. Most tiers allow swimming, shared with schools of soro brook carp that treat human feet as an exfoliation buffet. The park named the top tier for Erawan, the three-headed elephant of Hindu myth, whose shape the final formation is said to resemble. Go early, climb straight to the top tiers first, and work your way down as the day-trip crowds fill the bottom ones.

## Best Time to Visit

November-April for the clearest, bluest water (rainy season churns it); weekday mornings are far quieter. The trail's upper half closes mid-afternoon.

## Getting There

About 65km north of Kanchanaburi town — local buses run to the park entrance in about 1.5 hours, or drive; Kanchanaburi itself (and its Bridge on the River Kwai) is 2-3 hours from Bangkok.
