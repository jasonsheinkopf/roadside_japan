Mount Io, one of Shiretoko's volcanoes, leaks hot spring water directly into a mountain stream — so the Kamuiwakka river runs warm, and the only way to experience it is to walk up the middle of it.

## Why It's Interesting

This is as close as Japan gets to a natural water park: a 500-meter wading route up a volcanically heated river inside a UNESCO World Heritage wilderness, scrambling over three waterfalls with the water getting warmer at every step — lukewarm at the trailhead, a proper bath-like 38°C at the fourth fall, where steaming spring sources vent above you. The whole thing is managed like the serious backcountry site it is: online time-slot reservations, a mandatory safety video, free loaner helmets, and rentable aqua shoes, because the mineral-slicked rock is genuinely slippery and the water acidic enough to sting a fresh scrape. Between the gravel-road approach, the bears-live-here signage, and the absurd novelty of climbing a warm waterfall, it's the single most memorable half-day in Shiretoko for most visitors who do it.

## Best Time to Visit

The river is open July through September only — August is peak, and during the Obon window (August 8-15, 2026) private cars are barred and a shuttle runs from the Shiretoko Nature Center instead.

## Getting There

Base yourself in Utoro on the peninsula's west coast; the trailhead is about 40 minutes from the Shiretoko Nature Center along an unpaved road (shuttle-only during peak August dates). Reserve your slot and rental shoes before you come.
