At the very top of the **Nasu Yumoto** onsen town, where the road gives way to steaming hillsides, stands a weathered wooden bathhouse that has been in business, in one form or another, for around **1,300 years**: **Shika-no-yu**, the oldest hot spring in Nasu.

## Why It's Interesting

Step inside and the centuries fall away. Bathers soak in a row of **cypress tubs filled with milky, sulfurous water** kept at a ladder of rising temperatures, working their way from the merely warm to the genuinely fierce. There are no jets, no music, no modern spa polish — just hot mineral water, old wood, and a ritual that has barely changed since travelers first found the spring. Legend says a wounded deer revealed its healing powers, which is where the name ("deer hot spring") comes from.

## Best Time to Visit

**Year-round**, and most blissful on a **cold, snowy day**, when the steam and heat feel earned.

## Getting There

Bus from **Kuroiso Station** up to **Nasu Yumoto Onsen**, then a short walk. Bring your own towel and bathe the traditional way.
