Some places are famous for one photograph. Phraya Nakhon's is a small golden-roofed pavilion standing alone in a vast cave chamber, lit by a single beam of sun from a hole in the mountain — and unlike most one-photograph places, it's even better in person, because you have to earn it.

## Why It's Interesting

The cave's ceiling collapsed ages ago, opening a skylight that lets a garden grow on the chamber floor. In 1890, the Khua Kharuehat pavilion was built here for a visit by King Rama V — a four-gabled royal pavilion in the middle of a mountain — and subsequent kings signed the cave wall. Around 10-11:30 each morning, the sun aligns with the ceiling hole and drops a theatrical spotlight directly onto the pavilion's roof. There is no road: you walk over a jungle headland from Bang Pu beach (or take a longtail boat around it), then climb a steep rocky 430-meter trail into the mountain. The effort filters the crowds to a trickle.

## Best Time to Visit

Arrive at the trailhead by about 9am so you're in the chamber for the 10-11:30am sunbeam window; November-February offers the coolest hiking weather.

## Getting There

Inside Khao Sam Roi Yot National Park, about an hour south of Hua Hin by car; park (or boat) at Bang Pu beach, then hike.
