Inside Asahi Beverages' **Gunma Factory** in Tatebayashi is a small, free museum built around a single product: **Calpis**, the milky, lightly fermented soft drink invented in Japan in 1919. The "Mirai" ("future") Museum walks visitors through that history and the modern production line before ending, appropriately, with a taste of what was just made.

## Why It's Interesting

It's a rare case of a company giving an entire museum to one drink — from the 1919 origin story and the evolution of the trademark polka-dot bottle and calf mascot, through to the working fermentation tanks that still make Calpis today. The tour is aimed at families and kids but doesn't feel like a sales pitch; it's closer to a company being genuinely proud of its own history.

## Best Time to Visit

Open year-round aside from year-end/New Year and other designated closures — there's no seasonal reason to time a visit, so plan around when you can get a reservation.

## Getting There

The factory is in Tatebayashi, in eastern Gunma near the Ibaraki border, about a 15-minute walk from Watase Station on the Tobu Sano Line, or a short taxi ride from Tatebayashi Station. **Tours are reservation-only** and fill up — book through the official site as soon as the next month's slots open. It's roughly a 20–25 minute drive from the **Yakult Ibaraki Factory**, another free tour in the same general area, making the two easy to combine in one day.
