## The temple and the folktale

Morinji Temple was built in 1426 as a Zen temple in Tatebayashi, Gunma Prefecture. It's the setting of Japan's beloved folktale *Bunbuku Chagama*, which tells the story of a sentient tea kettle and a tanuki (raccoon dog) that travels across Japan performing acrobatic stunts and tightrope walking to fund the temple's repair.

## What to see

**Sanmon (gate)**: Thatched wooden structure from 1694.  
**Hondo (main hall)**: Thatched wooden building from 1468.  
**The tea kettle**: The legendary Bunbuku Chagama itself — purple gilt bronze, 11.2kg, on display (300 yen entry fee).  
**Tanuki statues**: 21 raccoon dog figures line the temple approach, a whimsical welcome tied to the folktale.

## Getting there

10–15 minute walk from Morinji-mae Station on the Tobu-Isesaki Line.

## Nearby

Tatebayashi is known for its regional udon, including mayudama udon made with silk powder from silkworm cocoons — a local specialty inspired by the temple's Bunbuku Chagama lore.
