In the center of **Erbach im Odenwald**, inside a Baroque castle on the town's own Marktplatz, sits the **Deutsches Elfenbeinmuseum** — the only museum in Europe dedicated entirely to the art of ivory carving.

## Why It's Interesting

In the last quarter of the 18th century, Count Franz I zu Erbach-Erbach introduced ivory to the bone and horn turners of his residence town. The craft took root so completely that Erbach became known as Germany's "ivory city" and grew into one of Europe's most important centers of ivory carving — a tradition still practiced by local workshops today.

The museum itself reopened in **2016** inside the former side and kitchen wings of **Erbach Castle**, with a modern, three-level exhibition built into the historic, protected structure without altering it — including a lift connecting all three floors, so the collection is fully step-free. More than **2,000 objects** trace ivory carving from the Middle Ages to the present, drawing on European, African, Asian, and Greenlandic traditions: intricately carved figures, miniature ships, and devotional objects, some barely larger than a coin.

## Best Time to Visit

April through October, when the museum keeps its longest hours (10:00–17:00, Tuesday–Sunday). It closes for a winter break from January 1 through early April, and keeps shorter hours (11:00–16:00) in March, November, and December.

## Getting There

The museum sits at **Marktplatz 7, 64711 Erbach im Odenwald**, in the town center. Erbach has its own stop on the **Odenwaldbahn** regional rail line, about a 10-minute walk from the castle. Old-town parking is limited, with a larger public lot near the edge of the pedestrian zone.
