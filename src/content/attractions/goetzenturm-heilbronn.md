The **Götzenturm** is a 30-metre sandstone tower on the south-west corner of Heilbronn's old city wall, built in **1392**, at **Allerheiligenstraße 1**. It is probably built from stones of the Klingenberg castle demolished in 1361.

## Why It's Interesting

It is named after the knight **Götz von Berlichingen**, but he never sat in it. He spent a single night in 1519 in the cruder **Bollwerksturm**. After Goethe's play *Götz von Berlichingen* (1773), Heilbronn simply decided the more impressive tower was his prison — and the name stuck.

On its roof, a paper-cut-out-style figure titled **"Über dem Abgrund"** ("above the abyss") by artist **Hubertus von der Goltz** has balanced at the edge since **1985**.

## Best Time to Visit

Daytime, when the tourist office is open to issue the tower key. Clear days make the climb worthwhile for the view.

## Getting There

Central Heilbronn, a short walk from the old town. Collect the key from the Tourist-Information Heilbronn or the city archive before climbing; confirm current hours and terms.
