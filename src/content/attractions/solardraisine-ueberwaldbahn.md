Between the villages of **Mörlenbach** and **Wald-Michelbach**, deep in the wooded Odenwald, a century-old rail line that lost its trains now carries something stranger: the **Solardraisine Überwaldbahn**, a fleet of self-pedaled, solar-assisted rail carts.

## Why It's Interesting

The **Überwaldbahn** line ran regular trains for most of a century before service ended in **1994**. Rather than let the heritage-protected, roughly **10-kilometer** track go to waste, the route reopened in **2013** for **26 solar draisines** — hybrid rail carts with a solar panel on the roof feeding an electric motor that assists on the inclines, alongside foot pedals for the rest. Each cart seats up to eight, tops out at **15 km/h**, and follows the single-track line past **four viaducts, two tunnels, and the Mackenheimer quarry** near Abtsteinach — a genuinely scenic slice of the Odenwald that regular traffic never sees. Because the line is single-track, carts travel in a fixed order with a mandatory safety gap, so it runs as an orderly convoy rather than a free-for-all.

## Best Time to Visit

Spring through autumn, during the seasonal operating window — the route runs a fixed daily schedule of tours, so book ahead rather than showing up and hoping for a spot.

## Getting There

Tours start at the **Draisinenbahnhof** in Mörlenbach, **Weinheimer Straße 16, 69509 Mörlenbach**, a short walk from Mörlenbach's own stop on the **Weschnitztalbahn** regional line (Weinheim–Fürth). A round trip with the stopover in Wald-Michelbach runs about 3.5–4 hours.
