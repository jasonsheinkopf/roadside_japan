The third recommendation in one go from **Ishimura** at Kita Kouboh — the coffee roastery already on this map — passed along from a returning traveler. It's a hilltop observation park on Biei's back roads, roughly halfway between JR Biei and JR Bibaushi stations.

## Why It's Interesting

Built on a ridge at about 300 meters elevation, the park gives an open, unobstructed 360-degree view: the Tokachidake mountain range rises to the east, red-roofed farmhouses dot the rolling hills below, and the west-facing side is known locally as one of the best sunset spots in the area. From July through September, the grounds fill with salvia and sunflowers, adding color to what's otherwise a straightforward big-sky viewpoint.

## Best Time to Visit

Summer through early autumn (roughly July–September) for the flowers; the mountain view itself is open year-round, weather permitting, though the access road can close in heavy winter snow.

## Getting There

A free on-site lot holds about 30 cars, plus a public restroom. There's no direct bus — it sits roughly midway on the hill-touring route connecting JR Biei and JR Bibaushi stations, so a rental car or bicycle is the practical way in, and it pairs easily with the Seven Stars Tree and Ken and Mary Tree on the same Biei hill loop.
