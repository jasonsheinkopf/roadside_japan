In Asahikawa's Shunkō district stands a red-brick building from 1902 that once served as the **7th Division's officers' social club** — and is today Asahikawa's only building with **National Important Cultural Property** status. Since 1994 it has held the **Nakahara Teijirō Memorial Asahikawa City Sculpture Museum**.

## Why It's Interesting

The museum is built around the life and work of **Teijirō Nakahara**, a sculptor from Asahikawa who died young at 32 but left a body of work heavily shaped by his study of **Auguste Rodin**. Twelve of Nakahara's own pieces are on permanent display alongside works by Rodin and other artists who influenced or were influenced by him, plus rotating special exhibitions. The building itself does a lot of the storytelling: its Western-style architecture, preserved through the 1994 renovation into a museum, is as much the draw as the sculpture inside.

## Best Time to Visit

Open year-round, 9:00–17:00 (last entry 16:30); closed Mondays (next day if a holiday) and over New Year's, but open daily through July and August.

## Getting There

In central Asahikawa's Shunkō district, reachable by local bus from JR Asahikawa Station or a short drive; a small on-site lot has about 7 spaces.
