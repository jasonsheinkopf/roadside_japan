Rising four floors over the Meguro River in Nakameguro is **Starbucks Reserve Roastery Tokyo**, one of only a handful of Reserve Roasteries in the world and, by most measures, the largest. It opened on **28 February 2019**, designed by architect **Kengo Kuma**, whose signature timber lattice work wraps the building in a wave of pale wood meant to echo the cherry trees lining the river outside.

## Why It's Interesting

This isn't a normal Starbucks — it's closer to a four-story theme park built around coffee. The ground floor houses **Princi**, a Milanese bakery; the second is a **Teavana** tea room; the third is the **Arriviamo** bar, serving coffee-based cocktails; and the top floor is a working **roasting floor**, where beans are actually roasted on-site behind glass, with the aroma piped through the whole building via a network of copper pipes overhead. It's a genuinely striking piece of architecture as much as it is a café.

## Best Time to Visit

It's open daily, but weekends draw long lines just to get in the door. A weekday morning is the easier visit. Spring (late March–early April) adds the bonus of cherry blossoms along the Meguro River right outside.

## Getting There

About a 7–8 minute walk from **Nakameguro Station** (Tokyu Toyoko Line / Tokyo Metro Hibiya Line), along the river — pleasant on foot, with minimal parking nearby for anyone driving.
