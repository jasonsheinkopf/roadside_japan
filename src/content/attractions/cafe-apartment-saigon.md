## An apartment block that turned into a coffee city

At 42 Nguyễn Huệ, right on Ho Chi Minh City's pedestrian boulevard, stands a nine-storey apartment block built in the mid-1960s — originally housing for government and military personnel, then home to shipyard workers after 1975. By the early 2000s it was neglected; around 2015, young entrepreneurs began renting the old flats and the building reinvented itself. Today more than a few dozen independent cafés, boutiques, and creative studios fill its apartments, and the balcony-stacked facade of competing signboards has become one of Saigon's most recognizable sights.

## Why It's Interesting

Most cities renovate buildings like this into a mall. Saigon did something better: it left the bones alone and let the cafés move in one apartment at a time. Exploring is the point — take the stairs, wander the corridors, and open doors. Every floor has its own character, and most have a coveted street-facing wing where balcony tables overlook the walking street. The ground floor houses a large bookstore, and the building's little old lift still runs (there's a small fee, which upstairs cafés often knock off your bill).

## Best Time to Visit

Late afternoon into evening. Nguyễn Huệ boulevard fills with strolling families and street performers after dark, and a balcony seat during that transition is the whole show.

## Getting There

It's on the Nguyễn Huệ pedestrian boulevard in District 1, opposite the Saigon riverfront end, steps from the Opera House metro station on Line 1.
