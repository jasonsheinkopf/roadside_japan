Every Japanese city has a local dish; **Utsunomiya** built an identity. The Tochigi capital is, by its own proud reckoning, the **gyoza capital of Japan**, locked in a good-natured national rivalry with Hamamatsu over which city eats the most **dumplings** per household.

## Why It's Interesting

Around the station, **dozens of specialist gyoza shops** turn out plate after plate of crisp, juicy dumplings — pan-fried, boiled, and deep-fried — for a few hundred yen apiece, a cheap and joyful grazing crawl. The city leans all the way in, right down to a slightly absurd, much-photographed **stone Gyoza Statue**: a figure demurely wrapped in its own dumpling skin. It's food as civic pride, and it's delicious.

## Best Time to Visit

**Year-round** — it's a city of restaurants. Come hungry and visit two or three shops to compare styles.

## Getting There

Dead easy: the gyoza shops **cluster around Utsunomiya Station**, a shinkansen stop, and pair perfectly with the nearby Oya stone sights.
