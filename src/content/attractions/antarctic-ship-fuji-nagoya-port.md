Moored permanently at **Garden Pier** in Nagoya Port, the **Fuji** is a genuine retired Japanese Antarctic icebreaker — not a replica, not a model, the actual ship — open to the public as a walk-through museum since 1985.

## Why It's Interesting

Built in 1965, the Fuji served eighteen years as Japan's second Antarctic observation ship, breaking ice to resupply Shōwa Station and ferry researchers south under the Japanese Antarctic Research Expedition (JARE). After decommissioning, she was preserved essentially as-is at Nagoya Port rather than gutted for a generic exhibit hall: the bridge, sick bay, barbershop, crew mess, and bunk rooms all remain in their working configuration, alongside a cavernous helicopter hangar. It's a rare chance to walk through a real polar research vessel, top to bottom, for the price of a coffee — ¥300 admission, unchanged for years.

## Best Time to Visit

Open year-round; the only closed day is Monday (bumped to the next day when Monday's a holiday), and it stays open through Japan's school breaks. No particular season matters more than another — everything worth seeing is indoors or on deck.

## Getting There

About 5 minutes on foot from **Nagoyakō Station**, served by both the Meijo subway line and the Aonami Line, at Garden Pier — the same waterfront complex as the Nagoya Port Aquarium and Nagoya Maritime Museum, making it an easy add-on for anyone already headed to the port.
