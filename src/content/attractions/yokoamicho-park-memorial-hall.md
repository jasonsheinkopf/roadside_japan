A green, unassuming park a few minutes from the sumo stadium that turns out to sit on one of the heaviest pieces of ground in Tokyo.

## Why It's Interesting

On September 1, 1923, the Great Kanto Earthquake struck at midday, just as people across the city were cooking lunch over open flames; the fires that followed merged into a firestorm. Tens of thousands of people had gathered on this exact plot — then a former army clothing depot, seen as an open, safe space — and an estimated 44,000 of them were killed here when the firestorm swept through. The Memorial Hall (Ireido) built afterward holds the ashes of roughly 58,000 earthquake victims citywide, and was later expanded to also hold the remains of 105,000 people killed in the March 1945 U.S. firebombing of Tokyo. It's an unusual, weighty kind of stop for a Saturday walk — a direct, physical link to two of the deadliest events in the city's modern history, and a quietly resonant one given the fire-safety education center waiting at the end of the route.

## Best Time to Visit

Any time — it's outdoor and free, though a memorial service each September 1st draws visitors specifically for the anniversary.

## Getting There

About five minutes from Ryogoku Station, sharing a corner with Kyu-Yasuda Teien and the Japanese Sword Museum.
