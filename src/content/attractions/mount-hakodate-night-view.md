Every Japanese city claims a night view; Hakodate's is one of the canonical three, and the reason is pure geography.

## Why It's Interesting

The city occupies a sandbar isthmus barely a kilometer wide, strung between Mount Hakodate and the mainland with Hakodate Bay on one side and the Tsugaru Strait on the other. From the 334-meter summit — three minutes up by ropeway — the streetlights fill that hourglass of land precisely to its edges, so the city appears as a luminous ribbon laid between two absolutely black seas. The Michelin Green Guide gives the panorama its top three-star rating, and the "million-dollar night view" nickname has stuck for a century. It's the least hidden gem in Hokkaido, and it earns the crowds: the moment in twilight when the lights come up while the sky is still deep blue is genuinely one of Japan's great sights.

## Best Time to Visit

Any clear evening year-round; summer is the comfortable season, with August sunsets around 18:40 and the ropeway running to 22:00. Come 30-45 minutes before sunset for the twilight transition.

## Getting There

Take the Hakodate city tram to Jujigai and walk about 10 minutes uphill through Motomachi to the ropeway base. Note the summit road bars private cars on summer evenings — the ropeway is the way up.
