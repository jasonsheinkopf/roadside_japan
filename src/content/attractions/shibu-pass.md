At 2,172 metres above sea level, **Shibu Pass** (渋峠, *Shibu-tōge*) carries National Route 292 over the shoulder between Mt. Yokote and Mt. Kusatsu-Shirane — the highest point reached by any national highway in Japan. It sits directly on the border between **Nakanojo, Gunma** and **Yamanouchi, Nagano**; the Shibutoge Hotel at the pass is famously built two-thirds in one prefecture and one-third in the other, with a line marked across the floor.

## Why It's Interesting

Because the road climbs so high above the surrounding valleys, cold mountain air often traps a layer of cloud beneath the ridge on calm mornings — the phenomenon known in Japan as **unkai** (雲海, "sea of clouds"). Stand at the pass at sunrise and the valley you drove up through can have vanished entirely under a flat white expanse, with only the highest peaks — sometimes as far as Mt. Fuji, Lake Suwa, and the Northern Alps — poking through.

## Best Time to Visit

The pass is snowed shut roughly **mid-November through late April**. Within the open season, dawn sea-of-clouds views are reported most consistently from **September into early November**, as the season's first strong temperature inversions set in before the road closes for winter — good news for anyone planning a fall trip like this one.

## Getting There

Route 292 is a mountain road with no rail or bus service; you'll need a car. It's part of the wider Shiga Kōgen / Shiga-Kusatsu-kōgen route linking Gunma and Nagano, and it's roughly a 40-minute drive down the mountain to **Jigokudani Monkey Park**, making the two an easy pairing on the same trip. Check road-closure status before heading up, since conditions can change quickly at this altitude even outside the official winter closure.
