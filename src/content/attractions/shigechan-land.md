**Shigechan Land** is a private open-air art museum on a former pasture in Aioi, a rural district of Tsubetsu, Hokkaido, along National Route 240 on the way to Lake Akan. Since 2001, illustrator **Shigenari Onishi** — a Tsubetsu native who studied at the School of Visual Arts in New York and later designed album covers in Tokyo for artists including Herbie Hancock and Ryuichi Sakamoto — has been converting roughly 8,000 tsubo (about 2.6 hectares) of the site into fourteen small exhibition buildings, each named after a part of the body (the Nose house, the Mouth house, the Ear house, and so on), as if the whole property were conceived as one living organism.

## Why It's Interesting

Every sculpture on the grounds is built from things that were once alive or once useful — driftwood pulled from the coast, scrap wood and metal, animal bones — reassembled into gangly, alien-looking creatures. In the Mouth house, a small cast of lanky figures called "Nano-Nanoian," with coconut-shell heads and narrow driftwood limbs, greets visitors. It's the rare roadside stop that's genuinely outsider art rather than a photo-op sculpture park: Onishi himself is often at the ticket booth, still building.

## Best Time to Visit

Open **May 1 through October 31 only**, Wednesday through Friday (staying open on public holidays), 10:00–17:00 (10:00–16:00 in October). Closed the rest of the week and completely closed November–April. Visitor accounts describe it as a walking-course kind of visit, pleasant in the fresh green of early summer or the autumn foliage in October — plan around the limited open days either way.

## Getting There

Shigechan Land sits directly on Route 240, a few minutes from the roadside station **Michi-no-Eki Aioi**, roughly an hour by car from Memanbetsu Airport. The old Aioi rail line through this area closed in 1985, so there's no practical train access — a car is the only realistic way in.
