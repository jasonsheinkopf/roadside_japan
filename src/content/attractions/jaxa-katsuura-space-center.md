Under four enormous white parabolic dishes — the largest **20 meters** across — JAXA runs a working satellite-tracking station on a quiet hilltop outside Katsuura, and next to it, a small **free exhibition hall** open to walk-in visitors.

## Why It's Interesting

The antennas themselves are the draw: real, working satellite-tracking equipment, visible from the grounds, on a scale that's genuinely startling up close. Inside, the exhibits translate that into something a young kid can grab onto — a life-size satellite model, scale rockets and International Space Station models, and hands-on simulators including one that lets visitors "fly" the Hayabusa asteroid-sample mission. It's free, low-key, and rarely crowded, which makes it an easy add to a family day on the Boso coast rather than a destination that needs planning around.

## Best Time to Visit

**Year-round** — it's an indoor exhibition hall with an outdoor view, comfortable in any season, including the height of summer.

## Getting There

Walk-up access isn't possible; it's about 15 minutes by taxi or car from Katsuura Station, with free on-site parking. Groups of **9 or fewer can visit without a reservation**; larger groups or a guided walkthrough need advance booking, and guided tours run on weekdays only. It pairs naturally with the **Katsuura Undersea Park** observation tower on the coast below, for a full day mixing sea and space.
