Just off Route 1 in **Mariko**, the twentieth station of the old Tōkaidō highway, a wooden restaurant has been serving one dish, more or less unchanged, for over four hundred years. **Chōjiya** opened in 1596 as a teahouse for travelers walking between Edo and Kyoto, and it still serves the same **tororo-jiru** — soup made from grated wild mountain yam (*jinenjo*) mixed with bonito dashi and white miso, poured over barley rice — that made Mariko famous long before Shizuoka city grew up around it.

## Why It's Interesting

Chōjiya is the **oldest continuously operating restaurant in Shizuoka Prefecture**, and in 2022 the Japanese government registered its building as a **nationally registered tangible cultural property**. Its fame predates any of that paperwork: Utagawa Hiroshige painted it into his ukiyo-e series *The Fifty-Three Stations of the Tōkaidō* as the "famous teahouse" of Mariko station, the haiku poet Matsuo Bashō name-checked its tororo-jiru in a verse ("*ume wakana Maruko no yado no tororo-jiru*"), and the comic travelers Yajirō and Kitahachi stop here in Jippensha Ikku's Edo-period bestseller *Tōkaidōchū Hizakurige*. Few restaurants anywhere can point to a Hiroshige print, a Bashō haiku, and a national cultural-property designation for serving the exact same bowl of soup.

## Best Time to Visit

Open year-round, any season — the wild yam harvest that supplies the soup runs in the colder months, but the restaurant keeps tororo-jiru on the menu every day it's open.

## Getting There

The easiest route is by car or bus along old Route 1: from JR Shizuoka Station's north bus pool, a Chūbu-Kokudō-line bus takes about 20 minutes to the Marukobashi-iriguchi stop, right outside. A free lot on site holds about 80 cars. By train, the nearest station (Abekawa, JR Tōkaidō Line) is a fairly long 35-minute walk, so the bus or a car is the practical choice — useful to know for anyone, like the traveler who wrote in about it, driving through Shizuoka on the way to Nagoya or Kyoto.
