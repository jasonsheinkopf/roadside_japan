Just off Route 39 in Asahikawa's Nagayama district, **Otokoyama** — one of Hokkaido's oldest and best-known sake brands — opens part of its working brewery as a **free museum**, tracing more than 350 years of sake-making history.

## Why It's Interesting

Otokoyama's brewing history stretches back to the Edo period in Hyōgo Prefecture, but the brand moved production to Asahikawa in 1899 specifically to use the mineral-rich spring water flowing off Daisetsuzan. The museum shows genuine Edo-period brewing tools, documents, sake sets, and ukiyo-e prints depicting the trade, plus video on the modern brewing process — and during the winter production season, visitors can watch part of the process firsthand. Admission and tastings are both free, and roughly 200,000 people visit each year, making it one of the region's most-visited small museums.

## Best Time to Visit

Winter, during the active brewing period, when freshly filtered sake is available to taste; the museum itself is open year-round.

## Getting There

About a 20-minute ride on the Dohoku Bus from JR Asahikawa Station to Nagayama 13-chōme, then a few minutes' walk; on-site parking is available for those driving.
