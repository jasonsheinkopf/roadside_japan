Every morning except Wednesday, fishers and farmers set up stalls on a narrow street in old-town Katsuura and sell what came in on the boats or out of the ground that day. The **Katsuura Morning Market** has run continuously since **1591**, when the local castle lord opened it to trade fish and produce — making it, at over 400 years old, one of **Japan's three largest morning markets**, alongside Takayama and Wajima.

## Why It's Interesting

It's a working market, not a tourist reenactment: roughly 60–80 stalls of fresh seafood from Katsuura's own port, local vegetables, dried goods, and street food, run by the same families' descendants for generations. For a kid, it's a hands-on lesson in where food actually comes from — watching a catch get sold an hour after it left the water — wrapped in the kind of sensory overload (smells, samples, noise) that makes history feel alive instead of read off a plaque.

## Best Time to Visit

**Early morning**, before 9am, when it's liveliest and coolest — a good way to beat summer heat on a hot-weather day. It alternates streets by the day of the month and is closed Wednesdays and New Year's Day.

## Getting There

About a 10-minute walk from Katsuura Station. There's no dedicated market parking; the city's Tona or Idemizu municipal lots are about 5 minutes away on foot. Easy to pair with a walk out to **Cape Hachiman Park** afterward.
