The **Yokoteyama Skylator** (横手山スカイレーター) is an outdoor moving walkway — a 200-metre "sidewalk that climbs a mountain" — installed in 1967 on the slope of Mt. Yokote (2,307m) in the Shiga Kōgen ski area, straddling the Nagano side of the Gunma–Nagano border. Paired with a short chairlift, it carries visitors the rest of the way to the summit without a hike, making it one of the easiest ways in Japan to stand above the clouds.

## Why It's Interesting

Mt. Yokote sits right beside Shibu Pass, the highest point on Japan's national highway network, and shares its dawn sea-of-clouds (unkai) phenomenon: cold air pools in the valleys overnight and, on calm mornings, leaves the peaks poking through a flat white expanse below. The Skylator itself is also a small piece of infrastructure history — it's among the oldest moving walkways still operating anywhere. At the summit, Japan's first crumpet specialty cafe and a bakery that claims the title of Japan's highest sit next to a viewing terrace, so the reward for the ride up isn't just scenery.

## Best Time to Visit

The lift runs roughly late April/May through late October and closes for winter snow. Within that season, cloud-sea sightings are most reliable right at sunrise and tend to burn off within an hour or two — plan to be on the first lift up if that's the goal, and treat any time after mid-morning as scenery-only.

## Getting There

The base station sits at the "Nozoki" stop on Route 292, the same road and parking cluster as Shibu Pass — a car is effectively required, since there's no train and only a seasonal bus from Yudanaka Station. Once you're up, Jigokudani Monkey Park and Shibu Onsen are both roughly 30–40 minutes back down the mountain, making a cloud-sea sunrise here an easy anchor for a full day in the area.
