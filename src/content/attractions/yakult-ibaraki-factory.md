Yakult's **Ibaraki Factory**, in the small town of Goka near the Ibaraki–Saitama border, is one of several plants across Japan offering free public tours of how the probiotic drink is actually made — from the tanks growing its signature **Shirota-strain** lactic acid bacteria to the line bottling the finished product.

## Why It's Interesting

It's a straightforward, well-run factory tour rather than a polished attraction, which is part of the appeal: real cultivation tanks, a working bottling line, and displays on Yakult's history since 1935 and its packaging from dozens of countries. It's aimed squarely at families, and it delivers on that without much fuss.

## Best Time to Visit

Open year-round except Sundays and the New Year holiday — there's no seasonal draw here, so the timing question is really about getting a reservation slot.

## Getting There

Goka sits in a rural corner of Ibaraki near the Tone River; JR Kurihashi Station is about a 7-minute taxi ride away, but most visitors arrive by car. **Book ahead** — reservations open 60 days before your visit date and close at 4pm the day before (groups of 10 or more must call). It's about a 20–25 minute drive from the **Calpis "Mirai" Museum** in Tatebayashi, Gunma, which runs a similar free tour and pairs naturally with this one.
