**Shibu Onsen** (渋温泉) is a small hot-spring town in Yamanouchi, Nagano, at the base of the road up to Shiga Kōgen — the same mountain road that leads to Shibu Pass and the Yokoteyama Skylator. Its narrow stone streets are lined with wooden ryokan dating largely from the Taisho and early Showa periods, and its signature ritual is the **nine-bath crawl** (九湯めぐり): nine separate public bathhouses, each fed by a different spring, unlocked in sequence with a single master key.

## Why It's Interesting

Few onsen towns in Japan keep this particular tradition alive at this scale: guests of Shibu's ryokan are lent a key that opens all nine baths, and local custom holds that finishing the full circuit and praying at the town's Yakushi shrine wards off misfortune. Walking the lanes after dark — wooden buildings, stone paving, steam rising between them under paper lanterns — is as much the draw as the bathing itself.

## Best Time to Visit

Shibu is open year-round and works as a stop any time of day, but the lantern-lit streets are at their best after dark. Winter adds snow on the rooflines and pairs naturally with a cold-season visit to nearby Jigokudani, whose monkeys bathe the same way humans do here, just less voluntarily.

## Getting There

From Yudanaka Station (Nagaden Line from Nagano), it's a short bus, taxi, or roughly 20-minute walk into Shibu. By car, it sits directly on the route between Yudanaka and Shiga Kōgen — about 30–40 minutes down the mountain from Shibu Pass and the Yokoteyama Skylator, and a similarly short drive from Jigokudani Monkey Park.
