A short walk from the sumo stadium sits one of Tokyo's more specialized — and more quietly intense — small museums: a building devoted entirely to the katana.

## Why It's Interesting

The museum is run by the Society for Preservation of Japanese Art Swords, the organization responsible for keeping the centuries-old craft of sword-making alive and for certifying historic blades as art objects. What's on display isn't costume-shop steel — these are working masterworks of metallurgy and craftsmanship, some many hundreds of years old, lit and displayed like sculpture. It rewards close, slow looking more than almost anywhere else on this walk.

## Best Time to Visit

Any weekday-or-Saturday morning; check ahead if visiting Monday, since that's the museum's regular closed day.

## Getting There

About five minutes from Ryogoku Station's Toei Oedo Line A1 exit, sharing a block with Kyu-Yasuda Teien.
