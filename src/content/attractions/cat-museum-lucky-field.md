In a quiet residential corner of Nakahara-ku, Kawasaki, a small hand-painted sign reads **猫のミュージアム ラッキー・フィールド** — "Cat Museum, Lucky Field." Inside, every surface is cats: framed paintings, shelves of maneki-neko figurines, cat-patterned mugs, cat cushions — and, if you're gentle about it, real ones too.

## Why It's Interesting

The owner, **Yoshino-san**, has run Lucky Field for **16 years**. It isn't a commercial cat café so much as a personal project: she has long taken in stray and rescued cats, and built this space so visitors — adults and kids alike — could learn how to be around them respectfully. Pay a small entrance fee and she'll make you a cool drink; you're then free to sit, look at her cat-art collection, and spend time with whichever cats are in residence.

At her peak she cared for **17 cats**. Today there are three, and in her own words, all three are still wary of people. Early experiences shape a cat's whole relationship with humans, she says, and some of hers came from very difficult situations. Earning that trust back takes an enormous amount of one-on-one time — more than any single person can always give — so it happens slowly, if at all, and never on a fixed timetable. That honesty is part of what makes the place worth visiting: it isn't staged for cuteness, it's a real, ongoing rescue effort, and you're a quiet guest in it.

## Best Time to Visit

Hours are limited and can vary — **call ahead** (044-752-0083) before making the trip. Go in expecting a quiet, unhurried visit rather than guaranteed cat cuddles; the cats currently here are shy, and Yoshino-san's whole philosophy is not to force that.

## Getting There

Lucky Field sits on a residential street at **3-11-12 Ida, Nakahara-ku, Kawasaki** (postal code 211-0035), a short trip from central Kawasaki. There's no train station immediately at the door, so check a map app for the best current route, and consider calling ahead for directions along with your visit.
