The port city of **Sakata** grew rich shipping the rice of the fertile Shonai plain, and the most photogenic relic of that trade still stands: **Sankyo Soko**, a long row of **black-walled wooden rice warehouses** built in **1893** and in use for over a century.

## Why It's Interesting

The clever, beautiful detail is the screen of tall **zelkova trees** planted along the back of the warehouses — they shade the buildings from summer sun and buffer the winter wind, keeping the stored rice at an even temperature. The result is the town's signature image: dark, dignified storehouses behind a curtain of green (or gold). The complex appeared in the beloved drama **"Oshin,"** which made it nationally famous, and today it holds a small history museum and shops. It's a quick, pretty, easy stop.

## Best Time to Visit

**Year-round**, with the zelkova trees lush in **summer** and golden in **autumn**.

## Getting There

A short bus or taxi from **Sakata Station**, easy to pair with the city's old merchant houses and the Mogami River mouth.
