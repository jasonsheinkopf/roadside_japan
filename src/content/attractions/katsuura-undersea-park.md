Sixty meters off the coast of Katsuura, a narrow pier leads out to a tower that does the opposite of every other observation deck: instead of climbing up for a view, visitors spiral **96 steps down** to a room 8 meters below the surface of the open Pacific, ringed with windows onto the real sea. It's the largest underwater observatory of its kind in East Asia.

## Why It's Interesting

Most "underwater" experiences are aquarium tanks with filtered, controlled water. This is the genuine ocean — wild fish, real currents, whatever the sea happens to be doing that day — viewed through 24 windows in a tower standing on the seabed. Over 90 species have been logged here, from small striped fish to occasional larger visitors. It's compact enough to suit short attention spans, which makes it a good match for young kids, while still being a strange enough structure (a room at the bottom of the sea, reached by a spiral staircase) to hold adult interest too.

## Best Time to Visit

**Summer** is peak season for the calm, clear water that makes the view worthwhile — the entrance fee itself scales with water clarity, so a still, sunny day pays off twice. It's open year-round, weather permitting.

## Getting There

It's about a 15-minute walk from **Uebara Station** on the JR Sotobo Line, or a short taxi ride from Katsuura Station. Pair it with **Kamogawa Sea World**, a proper aquarium about 20 minutes south down the coast, for a full day on the Boso Peninsula.
