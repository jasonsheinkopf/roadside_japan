Right where Ueno Park spills down to Shinobazu Pond sits a museum with no grand facade and no gift-shop sprawl — just two floors built to recreate the vanished world of Tokyo's *shitamachi*, the low-lying merchant-and-artisan downtown that stood in contrast to the hilltop samurai districts.

## Why It's Interesting

The ground floor is the draw: a full-size reconstruction of a 1920s row house — a lantern shop out front, a picture-story (kamishibai) rental shop behind it — rebuilt down to the tatami, the tools, and the toys, and you're free to step inside and handle things rather than view them behind glass. Upstairs, photos and artifacts trace how ordinary shitamachi life shifted through the Meiji, Taisho, and Showa eras. It opened in 1980, years before the bigger, better-known Edo-Tokyo Museum, as part of a wave of local history museums trying to preserve a Tokyo that redevelopment kept erasing.

## Best Time to Visit

Any morning works — it's compact, indoor, and rarely crowded, which makes it a good first stop before a longer walk east. Good option if the weather turns, too.

## Getting There

A five-minute walk from Ueno Station along the edge of Shinobazu Pond, right at the southern tip of Ueno Park — an easy first stop before heading east toward Kuramae and Asakusabashi.
