South of Bangkok on the Sukhumvit road, a three-headed elephant the height of a fourteen-story building surfaces above the traffic — and it's hollow.

## Why It's Interesting

The Erawan Museum inverts the usual big-statue formula: the giant isn't just to look at, it's to enter. The 250-ton bronze Erawan — the three-headed elephant that carries the god Indra in Hindu mythology — stands on a pink, museum-filled pedestal; inside, a staircase winds up through one of the hind legs into the belly, where the ceiling opens into a stained-glass map of the cosmos. The pedestal below holds antiquities and Buddha images collected by builder Lek Viriyaphant, the same magnificently obsessive tycoon behind Pattaya's Sanctuary of Truth. Bangkok's BTS Skytrain even named the nearest station Chang Erawan — "Erawan Elephant" — after it.

## Best Time to Visit

Year-round and mostly indoors once you're inside the elephant — a good heat-of-the-day or rainy-season stop on the way between Bangkok and the coast.

## Getting There

Right on the BTS Sukhumvit Line at Chang Erawan Station, about 45 minutes from central Bangkok — one of the easiest big oddities in Thailand to reach by transit.
