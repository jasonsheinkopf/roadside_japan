## A tycoon's palace full of lacquer and light

The Ho Chi Minh City Museum of Fine Arts occupies the former mansion of Hui Bon Hoa ("Uncle Hoa"), a property magnate who was among the wealthiest men in Indochina in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Built between 1929 and 1934 as the family residence and real-estate office, the U-shaped, four-storey building blends French colonial grandeur with Chinese and Vietnamese detailing across roughly 4,000 square meters — sweeping staircases, stained glass, patterned tile floors, and courtyards.

## Why It's Interesting

The building comes with legends attached. It was designed with 100 doors — a feng-shui number of completeness — but the colonial authorities objected to one of them, so the family built 99, and "the 99-door mansion" stuck as its nickname. Opposite the main entrance hall stands a wooden elevator said to be the first assembled in Saigon, its cabin carved and decorated like an ancient palanquin. The museum itself holds a major collection of Vietnamese fine art, from ancient sculpture through the lacquer and silk paintings the country is famous for, including works chronicling the war years.

## Getting There

At 97A Phó Đức Chính in District 1, a couple of blocks from Bến Thành Market and its metro station. Closed Mondays, like most Vietnamese museums — check before you go.
