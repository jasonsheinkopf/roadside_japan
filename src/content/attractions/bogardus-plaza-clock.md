## The only hand-wound clock left in the city

The clock at Bogardus Plaza, where Chambers Street, Hudson Street, and West Broadway meet in Tribeca, is believed to be New York City's only mechanical, weight-driven sidewalk clock still running by hand rather than electric motor. At 1,500 pounds and roughly 17 feet tall, the cast-iron tower clock is wound weekly, the old-fashioned way, by volunteers from the Friends of Bogardus Plaza and serviced by the city's official clock master.

## From a Michigan storefront to Tribeca

The clock was originally installed outside a jeweler's shop in Lansing, Michigan, in 1910. Decades later it was acquired and restored by a clock collector in Ontario, Canada, then transported and reinstalled in its current spot when Bogardus Plaza was completed in 2020, a gift to the city from the nonprofit Friends of Bogardus Plaza.

## Named for a cast-iron pioneer

The plaza takes its name from James Bogardus (1800–1874), an inventor and architect who pioneered cast iron as a building material and, in 1849, built what's considered the first cast-iron building in the country at the nearby corner of Washington and Murray Streets. Two of his surviving cast-iron buildings still stand a short walk away, at 75 Murray Street and 85 Leonard Street. The plaza's thirteen granite "eggs" are a nod to the neighborhood's earlier life as Manhattan's wholesale "Butter and Egg" district.

## Visiting

The plaza sits right at the Chambers Street subway complex (A/C/1/2/3), making it an easy stop while exploring Tribeca. There's no cost and no set hours — the clock is simply part of the streetscape, ticking away on its own mechanical time.
