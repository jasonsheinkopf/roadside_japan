A small, easy-to-miss strolling garden just north of the sumo stadium — free, quiet, and older than almost everything around it.

## Why It's Interesting

The garden began in 1691 as the grounds of a samurai residence, its pond fed directly by the tidal pull of the neighboring Sumida River. Financier Zenjiro Yasuda, founder of the Yasuda financial group (an ancestor of today's Mizuho), remodeled the property in 1894; it was rebuilt again and opened to the public in 1927 after being damaged in the 1923 Great Kanto Earthquake. The river no longer feeds the pond directly, but the garden still pumps water in and out daily to mimic the original tidal rhythm — a small mechanical echo of three centuries of history.

## Best Time to Visit

Open year-round except for the New Year's holiday window (Dec 29-Jan 1); a calm five-minute stop any time of day.

## Getting There

Five minutes from Ryogoku Station's west exit, right beside the Japanese Sword Museum and Yokoamicho Park.
