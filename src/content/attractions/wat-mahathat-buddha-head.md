For four centuries Ayutthaya was one of the largest cities on earth; in 1767 it was burned and emptied in a single sacking. The banyan trees have been quietly reclaiming it ever since — and one of them picked up a Buddha.

## Why It's Interesting

Somewhere in the chaos of the city's destruction, a sandstone Buddha head came to rest at the base of a banyan at Wat Mahathat, the kingdom's holiest temple. The tree grew; the roots flowed around the head like slow water; and today the serene stone face gazes out from a living wooden frame, at ground level, in perfect condition while headless statues line the ruined galleries behind it. Nobody arranged it — the image that launched a million Thailand posters is an accident of botany and war. The surrounding UNESCO historical park rewards a slow half day by bicycle or tuk-tuk: collapsed prangs, rows of decapitated Buddhas, and brick foundations of what was once a city of a million people.

## Best Time to Visit

November-February for cool dry weather; early morning beats both the heat and the tour buses from Bangkok.

## Getting There

About 1.5 hours north of Bangkok on the ordinary train (astonishingly cheap), then a short tuk-tuk or rented bicycle ride into the historical park.
