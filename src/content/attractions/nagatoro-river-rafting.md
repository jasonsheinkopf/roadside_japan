Nagatoro, at the northern edge of the Chichibu basin, packs river rapids, swimming, and a striking rock formation into a single afternoon trip that's easily doable from Tokyo without an overnight stay.

## Why It's Interesting

Rafts here are steered the traditional way — by a boatman working a single long pole rather than paddles — down roughly 7km of the Arakawa River. The ride alternates between placid stretches, where passengers are encouraged to jump in and swim, and sudden rapids where the raft spins through fast water. Midway through, the river cuts past Iwadatami, a dramatic shelf of flat, tilted sedimentary rock officially recognized as one of Japan's Cultural Landscapes — a formation striking enough to be worth a stop on the riverbank on its own.

## Best Time to Visit

Operates from roughly March through early December, but summer (July-September) is peak season and the only time swimming stops are part of the standard trip.

## Getting There

About two hours from central Tokyo via the Seibu-Ikebukuro Line or Chichibu Railway to Nagatoro Station, then a five-minute walk to the rafting operators' offices.
