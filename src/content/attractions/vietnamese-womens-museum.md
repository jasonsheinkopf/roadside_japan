## The museum Hanoi visitors don't expect to love

Founded in 1987 and run by the Vietnam Women's Union, the Vietnamese Women's Museum at 36 Lý Thường Kiệt tells the country's story through its women — in family, in history, and in fashion. Its collection runs to roughly 25,000 artifacts, photographs, and personal items, and it consistently ranks among the best-reviewed museums in Vietnam.

## Why It's Interesting

Plenty of museums display things; this one displays lives. Exhibits move from marriage and childbirth customs across Vietnam's ethnic groups, to the wartime women who served as couriers, guerrillas, and "long-haired army" organizers, to the celebrated street-vendor exhibit — first-person accounts of the women who carry Hanoi's markets on their shoulders every morning. The top floor's costume gallery, including a striking áo dài collection and dress from many of the country's 54 ethnic groups, sends you out the door looking at the city differently.

## Getting There

It's in the French Quarter at 36 Lý Thường Kiệt, about a ten-minute walk south of Hoàn Kiếm Lake. Open 8:00–17:00, last admission 16:30.
