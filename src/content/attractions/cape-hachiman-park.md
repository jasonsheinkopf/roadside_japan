**Cape Hachiman Park** sits on the site of the former Katsuura Castle, a headland jutting out between Katsuura Bay and the open Pacific. What was once a fortress is now a free public park: an observation plaza looking out over the water, the old castle earthworks, walking paths, playground equipment for kids, and a bronze statue of **O-Man no Kata** — a concubine of Tokugawa Ieyasu, born on this cape, whose son became the founding lord of the Kishu Tokugawa line and whose grandson was the famous Mito Mitsukuni.

## Why It's Interesting

It packs three different things into one short visit: a genuine ocean view (Katsuura Bay, the Pacific, and the Katsuura Lighthouse across the water), a real if faint layer of history in the castle ruins and the O-Man statue, and playground equipment that makes it an easy stop for kids rather than a purely scenic overlook. That combination — history you can touch, a view worth the walk, and a place for a child to actually run around — is rare on a coastline mostly known for beaches and fish markets.

## Best Time to Visit

**Year-round.** It's an outdoor park with no seasonal closures; clear days give the best views across the bay, and the exposed plaza can be windy, so a light jacket helps even in summer.

## Getting There

About 10 minutes by car or taxi from Katsuura Station, or a flat 30-minute walk along the harbor. Free parking (roughly 30 spaces) is at the cape's tip, reached through a short tunnel past the fishing port — easier than the alternative route from the top of the hill.
