On a wooded ridge above the wine-growing town of **Abstatt**, in Baden-Württemberg's Heilbronn district, stands **Burg Wildeck** — a hilltop castle with a genuinely strange second act: a medieval stronghold that became a working laboratory for German wine.

## Why It's Interesting

Wildeck was likely built in the High Middle Ages by the Lords of Heinriet, then passed by marriage and sale through several noble families before landing with the Counts of Löwenstein in 1492. It didn't have an easy few centuries: the castle was destroyed during the **German Peasants' War** and later changed hands repeatedly through the **Thirty Years' War**, before Count Carl of Löwenstein finally reclaimed it after the Peace of Westphalia in 1653.

The twist is what happened after. In 1933 the castle and its surrounding vineyards passed to the state of Württemberg, and since **1940** it has operated as an experimental estate of the **Weinsberg wine school (LVWO)** — a real, ongoing viticulture research station cultivating grape varieties including **Samtrot** and **Dornfelder** on the same slopes where armies once fought over the hill. World War II severely damaged the historic buildings, so much of what visitors see today is more recent construction, but the site's odd double life — battle-scarred fortress turned grape-breeding lab — is still very much active.

## Best Time to Visit

Spring through autumn, when the surrounding vineyard rows are green or turning color and the ridge is easiest to walk. Winter access is possible but the unpaved path can be muddy.

## Getting There

Burg Wildeck sits above Abstatt at **Wildeckstraße 1, 74232 Abstatt**, on a promontory at the edge of the Löwenstein Hills. There's no train station in Abstatt itself — the nearest regional rail hub is Heilbronn, from which it's a short bus ride or drive into town, followed by a walk up the ridge.
