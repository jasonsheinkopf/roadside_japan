**Mount Omuro** (大室山, *Ōmuroyama*) is a near-perfect cinder cone rising 580 meters above the Izu Peninsula near Ito, Shizuoka — round enough, from a distance, to look almost artificial. It last erupted roughly 4,000 years ago, and its lava flows once reshaped the coastline below into what is now the dramatic Jogasaki Coast.

## Why It's Interesting

Mount Omuro is one of about 70 small, single-eruption ("monogenetic") volcanoes that make up the Izu-Tobu volcanic field, but it's by far the most visited: the entire mountain is a nationally designated Natural Monument, and visitors reach the top not by trail but by open-air chairlift — hiking the slopes is prohibited to protect the grass that blankets the cone. At the summit, a paved, roughly 1-kilometer promenade circles the rim of a crater about 300 meters wide and 70 meters deep, with panoramic views that stretch to Mount Fuji, Oshima Island, and the Izu Islands on a clear day. Down inside the crater bowl itself, visitors can try archery, and paragliders launch off the outer slope. Every February, on the second Sunday, the entire mountainside is deliberately set alight in the **Omuroyama Yamayaki** — a controlled burn with roughly 700 years of history that clears old grass in about 15 minutes flat and is best watched from Sakura no Sato Park at the base.

## Best Time to Visit

Year-round: fresh green slopes in spring and summer, and the mountain's silhouette is visible from most of the Izu Kogen area at any season. The Yamayaki burning festival, typically the second Sunday of February, is the single most dramatic day to visit, though the mountain looks scorched black for a few weeks afterward before greening back up.

## Getting There

The chairlift base station sits a short bus ride from **Izu-Kogen Station** on the Izukyu Line (about 20 minutes, toward "Shaboten Zoo / Mt. Omuro"), or roughly 40 minutes by bus from Ito Station. Free parking is available at the base for those arriving by car.
