Most temples are built by communities over centuries; the White Temple is one man's ongoing argument with eternity, and it looks like nothing else on earth.

## Why It's Interesting

Thai visual artist Chalermchai Kositpipat bought a decaying local temple in the 1990s and began rebuilding it as a personal offering — entirely white (the Buddha's purity), embedded everywhere with mirror chips so the whole structure glitters, and deliberately confrontational. You enter across a bridge over a pit of hundreds of sculpted, grasping hands; inside, the murals swirl demons, nuclear war, and pop-culture figures — Batman, Neo from The Matrix, Doraemon — into Buddhist cosmology. Even the restroom block is part of the art: cast in ornate gold as a pointed contrast to the white temple, worldly versus spiritual. Kositpipat funds it entirely himself to keep control, and the site is designed to keep growing long after him.

## Best Time to Visit

Year-round; go early morning for cooler air and the low sun blazing off the mirror inlay. November-February is northern Thailand's dry, pleasant season.

## Getting There

About 13km south of Chiang Rai city — 15 minutes by taxi or songthaew, and every Chiang Rai–Chiang Mai bus route passes nearby.
