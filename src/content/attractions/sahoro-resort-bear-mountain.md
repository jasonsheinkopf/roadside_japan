**Sahoro Resort Bear Mountain** (サホロリゾート ベア・マウンテン) is a 15-hectare fenced forest reserve at Karikachi Kogen, on Route 38 near Karikachi Pass in Shintoku, Hokkaido, billed as Japan's first free-roaming brown bear safari park. Roughly a dozen **Ezo brown bears** (Ursus arctos yesoensis) — Hokkaido's native brown bear subspecies — live and roam inside the enclosure, many of them transferred from an older bear farm, rather than being kept in cages for display.

## Why It's Interesting

Most Japanese "bear parks" put the bears in a pit or a cage; here the arrangement is flipped. Visitors ride a **Bear Watching Bus** — an open-topped vehicle with heavy iron bars — along a roughly 1.2 km route through the trees where the bears actually live, or walk the **elevated forest walkway**, a boardwalk about 370 meters long and 5 meters up, that ends at "Bear Point," a viewing area with reinforced glass reportedly just centimeters from where a bear might wander. The park was modeled on Sweden's Orsa Björnpark and is run by a company with decades of prior bear-park experience, and its stated mission is protecting a genetically distinct, at-risk local bear population rather than simply exhibiting it.

## Best Time to Visit

Strictly **seasonal**: the park opens late April and closes mid-October (2026 season April 25–October 18), 9:00 AM–4:00 PM with last entry at 3:20 PM (3:00 PM in October, for earlier sunset). It closes completely for winter, when Hokkaido's brown bears would be denning anyway. Operations can pause during storms or high wind for visitor safety.

## Getting There

The park sits on Route 38 in Karikachi Kogen, about 15 minutes by car from **JR Shintoku Station** on the Sekisho Line — there is no direct train or bus access, so a car (rental or taxi from the station) is the practical way in. Free parking is available for around 600 vehicles.
