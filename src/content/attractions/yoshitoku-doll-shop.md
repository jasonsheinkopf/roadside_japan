Asakusabashi is Tokyo's low-key doll capital — block after block of wholesalers selling ornate Hina and Gogatsu dolls — and Yoshitoku, trading continuously since 1711, is the district's anchor.

## Why It's Interesting

The shop received its original name from the sixth Tokugawa shogun in 1711 and has operated on essentially the same ground ever since, through name changes that eventually settled on "Yoshitoku" in 1873. What most visitors miss: the 4th floor holds a free exhibition room of dolls and toys collected by the shop's own 10th-generation head, a serious researcher of Japanese doll history — a small museum hiding inside a working store.

## Best Time to Visit

Anytime, though the shop (and the whole district) is at its most visually striking in the weeks before Hina Matsuri (early February into March) and Children's Day (late April into May), when elaborate seasonal doll displays fill the windows up and down the street.

## Getting There

About five minutes on foot from Asakusabashi Station, on the direct line of travel between Kuramae and Ryogoku.
