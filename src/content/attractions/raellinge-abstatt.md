Walk through **Abstatt**, a small wine-growing town in Baden-Württemberg's Heilbronn district, and you'll start noticing them: enormous, brightly painted cat sculptures perched on street corners, outside shops, and clustered thickest in the town's **Bürgerpark**. There are **39** of them, and every single one looks different.

## Why It's Interesting

They're called the **Rällinge** — "Rälling" being the local Swabian dialect word for a tomcat. The name comes from an old bit of local folklore: young men from Abstatt reportedly used to travel to neighboring villages to court women, meowing under windows to make their presence known, and were teased as "Rällings" — tomcats on the prowl — for it. About 40 years ago, a graphic artist named **Horst Schwarz** gave the character its now-familiar face. When Abstatt's **Bürgerpark (civic park)** opened in **2008**, the town turned the mascot into a public art project: two-meter-tall blank cat figures were offered to local clubs, schools, and businesses to design and paint however they liked.

The result is a town-wide open-air gallery with no two cats alike — polka-dotted, floral, striped, painted with local landmarks, sports-club colors, or pure abstract color. Locals treat spotting all 39 as a genuine local pastime, and the sculptures have become Abstatt's defining piece of civic identity — mentioned by name in the town's own tourism materials as *the* thing to look for.

## Best Time to Visit

Any time — the sculptures are permanent, outdoor, and free to view year-round. Spring and summer make for the most pleasant walking weather to hunt down cats scattered across town.

## Getting There

The heaviest concentration is in the **Bürgerpark**, at **Rathausstraße 30, 74232 Abstatt**, right by the town hall. From there, more Rällinge are scattered on ordinary streets throughout town — worth wandering rather than beelining. There's no train station in Abstatt itself; the nearest regional rail hub is Heilbronn, followed by a short bus ride or drive.
