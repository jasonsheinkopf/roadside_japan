Hokkaido isn't supposed to have tropical-looking water — which is exactly why the Shakotan Peninsula's electric turquoise sea earned its own named color, and why the ridge walk at its tip is one of the island's great short hikes.

## Why It's Interesting

From the trailhead gate, Charenka's Path runs about 770 meters along a knife-edge ridge to a small lighthouse at the very tip of Cape Kamui, with the sea falling away on both sides and a nearly 300-degree horizon. On a sunny day the shallows glow the famous "Shakotan Blue" — a mineral-clear turquoise unlike anywhere else on the island. The cape carries a heavy legend: Charenka, an Ainu woman in love with the warrior Minamoto no Yoshitsune, is said to have thrown herself into the sea here and become the offshore Kamui Rock, and for centuries the cape was forbidden to women (passing ships reportedly wouldn't carry them past it) until the ban ended in the Meiji era. In summer the same coastline doubles as Japan's most celebrated sea-urchin ground — June-to-August uni-don in the fishing villages is half the reason to make the drive.

## Best Time to Visit

June through August for the bluest water, calm winds, the gate's longest hours (8:00-17:30 in August), and uni season; the exposed trail closes in strong wind, so favor mornings.

## Getting There

About 2.5 hours' drive from Sapporo (1.5 from Otaru) along the coast road; by transit, JR to Otaru then the Chuo Bus toward Shakotan to the Cape Kamui stop (about 2.5 hours).
