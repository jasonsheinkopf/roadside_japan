On the southern shore of **Lake Akan** in eastern Hokkaido sits **Akan Ainu Kotan**, the largest Ainu settlement in Japan — a real, lived-in community of roughly 120 people across 36 households, not a re-created village.

## Why It's Interesting

The Kotan's main street is lined with **more than 30 shops and workshops**, most run by the craftspeople who made what's on the shelves: intricate wood carving and embroidery drawn from generations of Ainu design. At the far end stands the **Akanko Ainu Theater Ikor**, Japan's first theater built exclusively for Ainu performance, rotating three stage programs — including a traditional dance show that's part of a **UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage** listing — plus a longer narrative piece, *Lost Kamuy*. The settlement exists at all because of a single act of generosity: **Mitsuko Maeda**, steward of the Maeda Ippoen Foundation and known to the community as the "Mother of Akan," donated the land free of charge so Ainu families could keep living and working on it.

## Best Time to Visit

**Year-round.** Shops keep longer hours through the summer tourist season, and evening theater shows (some starting around 9pm) run most nights in high season; check the current schedule before dark.

## Getting There

Buses run from **Kushiro Station** and **Kushiro Airport** to Akanko Onsen, roughly 70-90 minutes; the Kotan's street starts a short walk from the town's bus terminal, on the same shore as the marimo boat pier.
