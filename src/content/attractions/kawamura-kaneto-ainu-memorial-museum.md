Tucked into the Hokumon-chō neighborhood of Asahikawa is **Japan's oldest Ainu museum** — a private family institution that has outlasted nearly every public one. It was opened in 1916 by **Kawamura Kaneto**, the eighth-generation head of a prominent Kamikawa Ainu family, and his descendants still run it today.

## Why It's Interesting

Kawamura Kaneto spent his career as a pioneering railway surveying engineer from the Meiji into the Shōwa era. When an eye injury forced his retirement, he put his savings into founding the museum rather than into anything for himself, determined to keep Kamikawa Ainu spiritual life, clothing, and daily tools from disappearing. The collection — roughly **500 artifacts** — sits alongside the surveying equipment Kawamura actually used, a rare pairing of Ainu heritage with the modern Japanese infrastructure his generation helped build. Outside stands a reconstructed **cise**, a traditional Ainu house thatched with bamboo grass, and visitors can sometimes try on Ainu clothing or catch an unscheduled live dance performance. The museum reopened in a renovated building in July 2023.

## Best Time to Visit

Open year-round; the outdoor cise and grounds are most pleasant outside the depths of winter.

## Getting There

The museum is about 4 km from JR Asahikawa Station — take bus 24 from stop 14 to the Ainu Kinenkan-mae stop (roughly 15 minutes), or drive; there's a free lot on site.
