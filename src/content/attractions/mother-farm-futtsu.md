There's a working ranch on a hilltop above the Boso Peninsula where the main event, once a day, is around 150 sheep running full-tilt down a hillside path while a crowd of visitors leans out of the way — and that's before the shearing show, the milking demos, and a slope of rapeseed flowers that turns the whole hill yellow every spring.

## Why It's Interesting

Mother Farm covers roughly 250 hectares on Kanozan, a hill overlooking Tokyo Bay, and structures the day around a rotation of animal shows rather than a static petting zoo. The signature event is the "Great Sheep March," where the farm's flock is driven at a run along a fenced route past the crowd — it doesn't run in the peak heat of July and August, when it's too hard on the sheep. A separate sheep-shearing demonstration shows a shepherd take a full fleece off in well under five minutes. Beyond the sheep, there are cows to milk, horses to ride, and alpacas and capybaras to feed, plus a tractor-train tour into areas the walking paths don't reach.

## Best Time to Visit

Nanohana (rapeseed) flowers cover the slopes from mid-February through mid-April, one of the farm's most photographed sights and a good reason to visit outside summer, when the sheep shows also scale back.

## Getting There

Take the JR Uchibo Line to Kimitsu Station, then a Nitto Kotsu bus from the station's south exit directly to the farm — buses run roughly every one to two hours on weekdays and hourly on weekends. The full trip from central Tokyo takes about two hours.
