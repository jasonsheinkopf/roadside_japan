## The last word in the desert

In 1944, radio evangelist and self-proclaimed medicine man Curtis Howe Springer filed mining claims on 12,000 acres of the Mojave and built the Zzyzx Mineral Springs and Health Spa — mineral baths, a church, a castle-like lodge, and an airstrip. He coined the name so it would be "the last word" alphabetically, and because it sounded like sleep.

At its peak it hosted 100 guests a night, drawn by Springer's radio-advertised miracle cures. The American Medical Association nicknamed him the "King of Quacks." In 1974 the Bureau of Land Management evicted him from land he never owned; today the site is the CSU Desert Studies Center, on the shore of Lake Tuendae.

## Visiting

- Reached via Zzyzx Road, a 4.5-mile dead-end exit off I-15 about 7 miles south of Baker.
- The research station grounds and the small spring-fed pond are accessible; be mindful it's an active field station.
- One of the great American place-names — the sign alone is worth the detour.
